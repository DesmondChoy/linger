# Proposed Muse guidance for iteration 20

Source freeze remains active. This document proposes two small additions to the existing reflection guidance; no source change has been made.

## Paraphrase fidelity

After the current sentences about who acts and attributing a character's justification, add:

> When paraphrasing a statement about one person allowing or requiring another to act, preserve who expresses the wish, belief or willingness and who performs the permitted or required action. Do not transfer a speaker's attitude to the person whose action they describe. Check the whole paraphrase against the source, even when a nearby quotation is exact.

Evidence: iteration19 Farm explicit uses an authentic Squealer excerpt but changes Napoleon being happy to let the animals decide into the animals being happy to decide. This is a subject-and-attitude error, independent of quote fidelity. The addition addresses nested statements generally and introduces no book, character or expected output.

The current personal-wording rule already says another person's description does not establish the reader knows or agrees with it and requires attribution instead of a first-person factual concession. Farm personal19 violates that rule. No duplicate personal-concession paragraph is proposed; the companion independent review must enforce the existing rule.

## Source-claim introductions

Within the existing complete-clause/sentence mapping paragraph, after “These spans are your claims, not quotations from the source,” add:

> Include an introductory summary when it states a source-based proposition, even if the supporting details appear in mapped bullets below. A heading or transition that only organizes the reply and adds no source claim needs no evidence mapping.

Evidence: root's saved release19 Pigeon case fails because the introduction asserts the Pigeon's accusation and two reasons but only the detailed reasons and conclusion are mapped. This is a real coverage omission, not a reason to exempt source-bearing introductions in Provenance. The proposed sentence makes the distinction without requiring every presentation span to be mapped.
